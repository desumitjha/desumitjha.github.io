Learning in public

Notes from my studies

Currently in Week 4 of Ed Donner's AI Engineer Core Track (LLM Engineering, RAG, QLoRA, Agents), alongside Python and SQL fundamentals.

Six AI benchmarks, six different exams

GPQA tests PhD-level science, MMLU-Pro general knowledge with ten answer options, AIME competition maths, LiveCodeBench fresh coding problems, MuSR reasoning over murder mysteries, and HLE the hardest mixed questions. A score is a report card for one subject, not a verdict on the whole model.

Open source vs open weights (FLUX as an example)

Some FLUX image models are fully open, some publish weights under a restricted licence, and the best is API-only. "Open" can mean very different things, so read the licence before building on a model.

Two ways to call Hugging Face models

Serverless inference is pay-per-use and quick to start but shared. A dedicated endpoint is your own rented machine: steadier and pricier. Choose by traffic and how much control you need.

NER and classification in three lines of code

Named entity recognition highlights people, places, companies and dates inside a sentence. Classification puts a whole text in one box, like positive or negative. The transformers pipeline runs both with small open models.
